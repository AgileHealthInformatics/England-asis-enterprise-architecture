An as-is enterprise architecture of the health service in England as commissioned, funded and overseen nationally. It contains a hierarchical business capability model, value streams, key business processes, a conceptual information model, stakeholders, application services and named information flows, with the relationships between them.

The scope is the enterprise, not solely the corporate body called NHS England. That distinction matters now more than usual, because the body is being dissolved while the capabilities persist and must be exercised by whoever holds them next.

Recorded in August 2026. The enterprise is mid-restructure: abolition of NHS England was announced in March 2025, integration into the Department of Health and Social Care is targeted for October 2026 subject to legislation, completion is expected around April 2027, regional teams are expected to become departmental outposts, integrated care board mergers and boundary changes took effect in April 2026 with more due in April 2027, and headcount across the national centre is being roughly halved. Every statement in this model about where accountability sits should be read as provisional.

This is an architectural reading rather than an official description, and it takes no policy position.

How to read this model

One observation shapes everything else here. The national enterprise does not operate the service it is accountable for. Care is delivered by around 200 autonomous trusts, roughly 6,000 general practices operating as independent businesses, community and mental health providers, community pharmacy contractors and a substantial independent sector. The centre influences delivery through contract, standard, payment, inspection and national digital service rather than through line management, so every capability in this model must be read as exercised across an organisational boundary rather than within one.

Two consequences follow. Information about a person is held institutionally and associated by identifier rather than shared, so the longitudinal view is assembled rather than stored. And the levers available to the centre act slowly, because a contract cycle rather than a management decision is the unit of change.

England's structural advantages are real and worth naming, because a model that only records weaknesses misdescribes the system: a single person identifier used across the estate, national electronic prescribing end to end in primary care, structured record transfer between practices, an unusually consolidated national workforce system, a mandated single clinical terminology, a national opt-out that is operationally applied, and a research infrastructure that is a genuine international asset.

Business Capability Model

Six capability groups, 34 Level 1 capabilities and 110 Level 2 capabilities, expressed as enduring organisational abilities rather than as functions, organisations or programmes.

Care Delivery Capabilities

IdentifierCAP-CD
LevelCapability group
Principal ownersProvider trusts; foundation trusts; primary medical contractors; community and mental health providers; independent sector

The abilities through which care is actually delivered to people. Almost none of them are exercised by the national body: they are exercised by around 200 trusts, roughly 6,000 general practices operating as independent contractors, community and mental health providers, community pharmacy and a substantial independent sector. The national enterprise commissions, funds, standardises and holds to account, which means its influence over delivery runs through contract, standard, payment and inspection rather than through line management. Understanding that is the single most important thing about this architecture: the centre does not operate the service it is accountable for.

Urgent and Emergency Care

IdentifierCAP-CD-01
LevelLevel 1
Capability groupCare Delivery Capabilities
Primary ownerAcute trusts; ambulance trusts; NHS 111 providers; urgent treatment centres; primary care out of hours
Value streams servedGet Help When Something Is Wrong, Approach the End of Life
StakeholdersNurses, Midwives and Health Visitors, Ambulance Clinicians, NHS Trusts and Foundation Trusts

The ability to receive, triage and treat unscheduled need across a tiered system running from digital and telephone triage through urgent treatment centres and same day emergency care to emergency departments and ambulance conveyance. The capability is the most visible measure of system health and the least controllable by any single organisation, because performance in emergency departments is determined largely by flow conditions elsewhere: bed occupancy, discharge readiness, community capacity and social care availability. Demand arrives through several front doors operating on different clinical records, so the same person may be triaged more than once with no shared account of what has already been decided.

StakeholdersDoctors, NHS Trusts and Foundation Trusts, Independent Sector Providers

The ability to deliver referred, scheduled care from outpatient assessment through diagnostics to procedure and follow-up. The capability carrying the largest single accountability pressure in the system, since elective waiting is the most politically salient measure the enterprise reports. It is also the capability most exposed to the separation between demand and capacity: referrals are generated by independent primary medical contractors, capacity is held by trusts and independent providers, and neither party sees the other's position without a national or regional intermediary. Independent sector capacity is used at scale, and episodes delivered there return as correspondence rather than as records.

Primary ownerGeneral practices as independent contractors; primary care networks; ICB primary care commissioning
Value streams servedGet Help When Something Is Wrong, Live With a Long-Term Condition, Approach the End of Life

The ability to provide first contact, continuing, comprehensive and coordinated medical care to a registered population. Structurally distinctive and architecturally decisive: general practice is delivered by independent businesses holding contracts, using clinical systems the NHS does not own, and holding the most complete longitudinal record of the population that exists anywhere in the system. Registration creates the list against which nearly all population accountability is calculated. The national enterprise influences primary medical care through the contract, the network arrangement, incentive schemes and system supplier assurance rather than through direction, which is why change here takes contract cycles rather than management decisions.

The ability to assess, treat and support people with mental illness across community teams, talking therapy services, crisis response, inpatient care and specialised provision, including care delivered under the Mental Health Act. Population and Prevention Capabilities

IdentifierCAP-PP
LevelCapability group
Principal ownersNational public health functions; UKHSA; local authority public health; ICBs; screening and immunisation programmes

The abilities directed at populations rather than at presenting individuals. Structurally split across organisational boundaries that do not align: health protection sits with a separate national agency, local public health sits with local government, screening and immunisation programmes are commissioned nationally and delivered through primary care and providers, and population health analysis sits with integrated care boards and the national centre. The split is a legacy of successive reorganisations rather than a design, and it means population capability requires coordination across statutory bodies with different accountabilities.

Access and Flow Capabilities

IdentifierCAP-AF
LevelCapability group
Principal ownersICBs; providers; national access functions; independent sector

The abilities that determine whether a person can get into the service they need and move through it. These are the capabilities on which the enterprise is publicly judged, and they are exercised across organisational boundaries by design: referral is generated in one organisation and received in another, capacity is held by several, and the person's position in a queue is a national statistic assembled from local lists. Access management is therefore an information capability at least as much as an operational one.

Value Streams

The outcomes a person seeks, expressed from their point of view rather than from the system's. Reading the capability model against these is the quickest way to see where the architecture is organised around institutions rather than around people.

Stay Well

IdentifierVS-01
Realised by capabilitiesScreening and Immunisation, Health Inequalities and Prevention, Population Health Management

Keeping people healthy through prevention, immunisation, screening and action on the wider determinants. The stream most dependent on parties the NHS does not commission, and the first to be deprioritised when financial pressure forces attention onto measurable short-term delivery.

Get Help When Something Is Wrong

IdentifierVS-02
Realised by capabilitiesAccess, Referral and Booking, Urgent and Emergency Care, Primary Medical Care, Patient Choice and Digital Access

Receiving a request for help through any channel, assessing it and directing it to the right service. Spans digital and telephone triage, primary care access and emergency presentation, and is the stream where the public most often experiences the system as fragmented.

Get Diagnosed

IdentifierVS-03
Realised by capabilitiesDiagnostics and Pathology, Access, Referral and Booking, Planned and Elective Care

Converting a clinical question into a diagnosis through assessment, testing and specialist opinion. The stream where waiting is most consequential clinically and where capacity constraints in diagnostics and reporting bite hardest.

Get Treated

IdentifierVS-04
Realised by capabilitiesPlanned and Elective Care, Mental Health Care, Medicines Optimisation and Pharmacy, Waiting List and Elective Recovery Management

Delivering the therapeutic intervention, whether surgical, medical, psychological or pharmaceutical, in the setting appropriate to need. The stream carrying the largest share of cost and the most visible waiting measure.

Recover and Stay Independent

IdentifierVS-05
Realised by capabilitiesCommunity and Intermediate Care, Patient Flow and Capacity Management

Rehabilitation, reablement and support to return to and remain in the community after illness or admission. Depends on joint health and social care capacity that no single organisation controls, which is why it manifests as delayed discharge rather than as a recognisable service failure.

Live With a Long-Term Condition

IdentifierVS-06
Realised by capabilitiesPrimary Medical Care, Population Health Management, Medicines Optimisation and Pharmacy

Continuing management of chronic illness through recall, review, self-management support and coordinated care. Predominantly a primary care and community stream, measured largely through incentive scheme indicators rather than through outcomes.

IdentifierVS-07
Realised by capabilitiesMaternity and Neonatal Care, Mental Health Care, Screening and Immunisation

Maternity, neonatal, child health and children's mental health care through to transition into adult services. Distinctive in being surveillance over years rather than episodes, and in changing provider, record and threshold at an age boundary.

IdentifierVS-08
Realised by capabilitiesCommunity and Intermediate Care, Urgent and Emergency Care, Primary Medical Care

Palliative and end of life care across settings, including advance care planning and coordination of the person's expressed wishes. The stream most dependent on information following a person between organisations out of hours, and therefore the clearest test of whether shared records work in practice.
